GDP Growth Trend

The Indian economy has demonstrated resilience, recovering from pandemic shocks to post strong growth driven by private consumption and capital formation.

GVA Composition (Current Prices)

Services continues to be the primary driver, but the Industrial sector has seen a resurgence due to PLI schemes and Capex push.

Macroeconomic Outlook

Drivers of Growth

  • Private Consumption: Rebounded significantly, acting as the main pillar of growth.
  • Capital Expenditure (Capex): Central Government's capex increased by 63.4% in the first eight months of FY24 compared to the same period in the previous year.
  • Housing Market: Sales have picked up, reducing inventory overhang and spurring construction activity.

Key Challenges

  • Geopolitical Tensions: Supply chain disruptions and volatile energy prices remain a risk.
  • Global Slowdown: Weak external demand affects merchandise export growth.
  • El Niño Conditions: Impact on agricultural output and food inflation remains a monitorable.

Fiscal Developments

Context: Reviewing the government's revenue and expenditure strategies. Focus is on the shift from revenue expenditure to capital expenditure to spur long-term growth.

UPSC Key Concept: Quality of Expenditure

The government is prioritizing Capital Expenditure (Capex) over Revenue Expenditure. Capex creates assets (roads, bridges) with a higher multiplier effect (approx 2.5x) compared to revenue expenditure (approx 0.9x), leading to sustained economic growth.

Prices & Inflation

Overview: Analysis of CPI (Consumer Price Index) and WPI (Wholesale Price Index) trends. Managing the "imported inflation" vs "domestic supply shock" dynamic.

CPI vs WPI Divergence

Insight: WPI has been in negative territory recently due to falling commodity prices, while CPI remains slightly elevated due to food prices.

Drivers of Inflation

1. Food Inflation

High contribution from Vegetables & Pulses due to erratic weather.

2. Core Inflation (Excluding Food & Fuel)

Has moderated significantly, indicating weak pass-through of costs.

Policy Measures

  • Open Market Sale Scheme (OMSS): Used to offload wheat/rice to cool prices.
  • Export Bans: Restrictions on Non-Basmati white rice, sugar, and onions to ensure domestic availability.
  • Monetary Policy: Repo rate maintained to anchor inflation expectations.
